## Recommended reconstruction order

### 01 — Foundation review (13 articles)
Settle the current world-level truths before doing mass downstream rewrites.

### 02 — Core species & peoples (12 articles)
Stabilise species first; then revisit dependent ethnicities/cultures.

### 03 — Eshil vertical slice (21 articles)
Eshil is the strongest existing network hub. Treat it as a coherent test region for the rebuilt Hesli.

### 04 — World systems & institutions (13 articles)
Languages, rituals, professions, ranks, organisations, laws and similar cross-cutting systems.

### 05 — Wider world & geography (11 articles)
Expand beyond the first coherent regional slice.

### 06 — People, history & story (14 articles)
Characters, myths, documents, conflicts, plots and narrative material after their foundations are stable.

### 07 — Objects, technology & ecology (0 articles)
Items, vehicles, technology, materials and conditions.

### 08 — Optional / future (138 articles)
Potential material and non-critical expansion. This is intentionally outside the critical path.

### 00 — Archive / archaeology (126 articles)
Old challenge/event/archive material. Keep it as an idea bank unless current canon gives it a reason to return.

## How I would use this

1. Pick a small number of P0/P1 items from **Foundation**, **Species**, and **Eshil**.
2. When a rewrite exposes a dependency, add that dependency to the queue instead of switching to a random unrelated article.
3. Do not let `Potential`, WorldEmber, SummerCamp, or archived material block the main reconstruction.
4. Preserve old material even when it is retired; it is still a source of ideas and historical context.
5. Treat this sheet as a living triage document. The automated classification is deliberately conservative where the export cannot reveal your current intentions.
